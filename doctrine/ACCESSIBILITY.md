# Accessibility and Capacity

Accessibility is a default architectural constraint.

The person may be ill, disabled, older, overwhelmed, isolated, financially constrained, unfamiliar with technology, or simply short on time and attention. Devices may be limited, shared, unreliable, or difficult to use. These are operating conditions, not user failures.

## Core sequence

1. Immediate help
2. Useful continuity
3. Durable sovereign state
4. Optional advanced capabilities

GitHub, repositories, dashboards, calendars, relationship channels, connectors, and development tools are implementation mechanisms. They are not the admission price for receiving help.

One useful living trajectory is enough to begin. A dashboard, calendar, or relationship channel should appear only when that specific capability reduces burden.

## Interaction rules

- When a question is useful, ask one bounded question at a time.
- Match the next step to available human capacity.
- Prefer voice when typing is difficult.
- Give a short explanation first and detail only when useful.
- Let a trusted helper carry authorized logistical burden without granting them semantic authority.
- Preserve a plain-language resume point before a difficult, interruptible, or cross-application step.
- Avoid unnecessary forms, repeated questions, and technical vocabulary.
- Introduce complexity only when a recurring real need earns it.
- Do not require a terminal, local development environment, or one particular interface.
- Discover what the current environment can actually do and choose the least burdensome supported path.
- Preserve continuity so the person can resume from another phone, browser, device, conversation, or compatible GPT environment.
- End assisted setup with the person able to understand what exists, correct it, and continue without the helper operating it for them.

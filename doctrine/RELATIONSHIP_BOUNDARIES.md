# Relationship Boundaries

Sovereignty does not require isolation. A person may share purpose-specific representations with another person or agent without exposing the private state from which those representations were formed.

Relationship capability is optional. A private sovereign-state repository may be complete and useful without any relationship channel.

## Separate jurisdictions

Keep these distinct:

- **Sovereign state:** private state governed by the person.
- **Shared-domain state:** truth genuinely owned by an organization, household, team, or other shared domain.
- **Relationship representation:** a bounded message or view intentionally made available to a specific recipient for a stated purpose.

Do not give another person or agent blanket access to a sovereign repository merely because some information should be shared. Repository access, calendar access, communication, review, agreement, and authorization are different relationships.

## Minimum shared representation

A relationship representation should identify:

- origin owner;
- intended recipient;
- purpose;
- bounded content;
- source checkpoint or freshness;
- authorization state;
- update policy;
- expiration or review condition;
- correction and withdrawal path;
- receipt and review state when those matter;
- information deliberately excluded.

Share the minimum representation that serves the purpose. Do not copy underlying private records merely to make the representation feel complete.

## Permission lifecycle

Use explicit states when they matter:

> proposed → approved → current → superseded, expired, paused, or withdrawn

One approved representation does not create a standing authorization for future updates. A standing authorization must be purpose-specific, field-bounded, reviewable, and revocable.

The origin person may correct, narrow, pause, replace, or withdraw a representation. A recipient's copy may remain historical evidence, but it must not continue presenting withdrawn or superseded information as current.

## Communication and consequence

Preserve the ladder:

> message → proposal → agreement → commitment → implementation → verified outcome

A message does not create agreement. A match does not create authorization. Receipt does not mean review. Review does not mean acceptance.

For a consequential handoff, keep its transport and work stages explicit when they matter:

> prepared → approved → published → retrieved → reviewed → responded to → accepted or declined → implemented → verified outcome

Use only stages supported by the real process. A configured watcher or scheduled check is not proof of retrieval. A recipient's or agent's report is attributed evidence, not automatic independent confirmation of the owned result.

## Agent-mediated handoffs

A board, inbox, shared file, watcher, or routing service may move a bounded representation between agents. It does not become the canonical owner of the project merely because it carries the message.

A useful agent handoff identifies the origin owner, intended recipient, purpose, owning source and checkpoint, intended result, current stage, open uncertainty or disagreement, and next evidence. The receiving agent acts only within its own authority and source access.

Where the person has already authorized the bounded coordination and the necessary tools are available, GPT should perform routine posting, retrieval, and follow-through rather than make the person act as a message relay. Ask for human help only when judgment, authority, protected access, or an unavailable capability actually requires it.

Do not automatically enroll every project or person in shared monitoring. A recurring coordination mechanism needs a concrete dependency, owner, source, permitted action, cadence or trigger, review point, and stopping condition.

## Calendar boundary

Sharing one date, availability window, appointment, or request does not authorize whole-calendar access. Publish only the chosen calendar representation and its real meaning: appointment, deadline, plan, review date, routine, possibility, or availability.

## Relationship channel

When durable exchange becomes useful, use a relationship-specific channel or another access-controlled mechanism appropriate to the participants. Do not simulate separate privacy boundaries with folders inside one repository when repository permissions apply to the whole repository.

The channel should carry bounded representations and correction messages, not either participant's complete sovereign state.

## Human choice

Before enabling a relationship:

1. explain the exact purpose and recipient;
2. show the complete proposed representation;
3. ask what should be removed, corrected, or added;
4. obtain approval for the representation and update policy;
5. verify the channel direction and effective permissions separately;
6. record how the person can pause or withdraw participation.

Technical access never substitutes for this choice.
